# Blood Bank Management System

The Blood Bank Management System is structured around several interconnected entities that ensure the safe collection, storage, and distribution of blood products. At the center of the system is the Donor, whose profile includes a unique ID, full legal name, biological blood group (such as A+ or O-), gender, and date of birth to verify eligibility. The system maintains their contact information via phone and email, records their physical address, and tracks their most recent contribution through the "Last Donation Date" attribute. This data feeds directly into the Donation entity, which logs every specific collection event. Each donation is assigned a unique ID and records the blood group, units collected, and the exact date of collection. Crucially, it tracks the "Expiry Date" to ensure safety and monitors vital health metrics like hemoglobin levels and blood pressure recorded at the time of the event.
To maintain operational efficiency, the Blood Inventory acts as a real-time ledger, identifying available supplies by blood group and tracking the total "Units Available." This inventory is updated whenever a donation is made or a request is fulfilled. On the demand side, the system manages Hospitals or Recipients, storing their facility names, locations, and primary contact details. These institutions submit a Blood Request, which specifies the unique Request ID, the hospital involved, the specific blood group and units required, and the urgency level—ranging from Normal to Emergency. The request's status is tracked through phases such as Pending, Approved, or Rejected.
Once a request is approved, the Blood Distribution entity records the final dispatch, noting the distribution date, the actual units sent, and the staff member who authorized the release. To close the loop and ensure a sustainable supply, the Donation Reminder entity automates donor retention. It identifies eligible donors based on their history, schedules a specific reminder date, and tracks the status of the notification (Sent, Pending, or Failed), effectively encouraging donors to return and contribute once they are biologically ready to do so again.
